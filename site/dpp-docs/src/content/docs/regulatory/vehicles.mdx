The End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 creates a passport of its own for vehicles: the **Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport**. It is not an ESPR passport, but it must work with them.

## When

- **Art. 13(1)**: "From 1 September 2032, each vehicle placed on the market shall have a Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport."
- The Regulation applies from 1 September 2028 (Art. 59(2)), with some provisions earlier. It repeals the End-of-Life Vehicles Directive 2000/53/EC from the same date, though parts of the Directive continue to apply until 31 August 2032 (Art. 57(1)).
- Its scope is staged (Art. 2(1)): passenger cars and light commercial vehicles first; special-purpose versions of them from 1 September 2029; buses, lorries, trailers and two- and three-wheelers from 1 September 2031.

## What it requires

- **Art. 13(2)**: the passport holds information on removing and replacing parts, components and materials; parts that still contain lead, mercury, cadmium or hexavalent chromium under a derogation; the declaration on recycled content; and the official spare-parts catalogue for the vehicle type. Access is free of charge.
- **Art. 13(3)**: information already available through another passport is not duplicated in the vehicle passport, provided the two are interoperable.
- **Art. 13(5)**: the information is based on open standards, interoperable, transferable without vendor lock-in, and machine-readable, structured and searchable.
- **Art. 13(6)**: implementing acts, due by 14 August 2030, set out access, technical design, storage, availability after the manufacturer ceases to exist, updates by third parties, and where the data carrier goes.

## How it connects to other passports

Art. 13(1) requires the vehicle passport to be aligned, interoperable and, where possible, integrated with other vehicle-related passports: in particular the **battery passport** under Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 Art. 77, the environmental vehicle passport under the Euro 7 Regulation (EU) 2024/1257, and passports under ESPR. An electric vehicle therefore carries a vehicle passport and a battery passport for its traction battery.

The Regulation also amends four other acts. For batteries, it replaces the table of restricted substances in Annex I of the Batteries Regulation, from 13 August 2026.

## Where the software stands

The software records the Regulation and its 2032 date in its instrument catalog, but models **no vehicle product group**: it cannot issue a vehicle passport. Whether one should exist before the implementing acts say what the passport looks like is an open question. Battery passports for electric-vehicle batteries are fully supported.
